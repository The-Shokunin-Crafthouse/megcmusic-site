Editing your website

Everything on megcmusic.com, and where to change it — Meghan Clarisse

The whole thing in one sentence. Every word and photo a visitor sees on megcmusic.com now lives in your WordPress dashboard. Edit it there, click Update, and the website rewrites itself — usually in about two and a half minutes. You never need to ask anyone.

01 How to change anything

  1. Sign in at admin.megcmusic.com/wp-admin
  2. Go to Pages and open the page from the table in the next section
  3. Scroll past the page's own content — your website fields are in labelled boxes underneath
  4. Change what you want and click Update
  5. Wait about three minutes, then refresh megcmusic.com

Every field has a grey line of help text under it telling you exactly where it appears. When in doubt, read that line — it was written for this.

03 Three things are shared — change once, changes everywhere

Your bio and pull quote live on the Home page. They appear both in Liner Notes on the home page and as "The Story" on the Press Kit. Edit them in one place; both update.

Your release list lives on the Music page. It feeds the Discography on Home, Music and the Press Kit, the Singles list, and each release's own page.

The site photo lives on the Home page, under Site Basics. It is the background on every page. Any single page can override it with its own "Photo for this page" — leave that empty and the page uses the site photo, which is what they all do today.

Because the site photo touches every page, check with Levi before swapping it.

04 Your release list, in a little more detail

This is the one field worth understanding properly, because it drives more of the site than anything else. On the Music page, under Your releases, each row is one record:

Kind
Albums and EPs go in the Discography. Singles go in the Singles list on the Music page. That is the only thing that decides it.
Its page on this dashboard
Points at the WordPress page whose text and lyric images become that release's "About the Record" page. Leave it empty and the release still lists — it just has no page of its own to click into.
Shop item
The product behind the "Buy" button. Leave empty if it isn't sold.
Spotify / Apple link
This record's own link. Leave either empty and the site falls back to your artist profile, set further down the same page.

Order. Both lists sort themselves newest first by the Year you enter, so you can add a row anywhere and it lands in the right place.

05 What is not a field — and doesn't need to be

  • Your shows — still added the way you always have, in The Events Calendar
  • Your shop products — still WooCommerce, unchanged, including prices and stock
  • Your photo galleries — the Media page pulls from your Photos page
  • Your sample set list — still its own page; the Press Kit links to it
  • Lyric-sheet descriptions — set on the image itself in the Media Library, not in the gallery field
  • The design — colours, fonts, spacing and layout are fixed. Nothing you type can break how the site looks.

06 If your change doesn't show up

Work down this list. It is almost always the first one.

  1. Wait three minutes and refresh. The site rebuilds itself after you save; it is not instant. Give it the full three minutes before deciding something is wrong.
  2. Force a fresh copy. Hold Shift and click reload — your browser may be showing you the page it already had.
  3. Open the page and click Update again. A second save re-sends the signal, and this fixes it more often than you'd expect.
  4. Tell Levi. Say which page you edited and roughly when. Nothing you can do in the dashboard breaks the site — the worst case is that a change sits and waits.

The site also rebuilds itself every night, so even a signal that goes missing entirely will have caught up by the next morning.

07 Two small habits worth having

Leave a field empty rather than deleting the box. Empty means "use what the site does today" almost everywhere — the Buy link, a page's own photo, a release's Spotify link. It is a safe thing to do.

Change one thing, check it, then change the next. Not because anything will break, but because if something does look off you will know immediately what caused it.
